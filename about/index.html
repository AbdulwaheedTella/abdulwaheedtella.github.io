About

Research with an eye on decisions

I build and test geospatial machine-learning models, and I care whether people can understand and trust what they produce.

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Civil Engineering at Monash University Malaysia, working on geospatial artificial intelligence (GeoAI) for flood susceptibility and urban exposure. My first degree is in remote sensing and GIS (FUTA, Nigeria) and my master's is in civil and environmental engineering (Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS), where I modelled air pollution in Malaysia with machine learning.

The problem I keep returning to is trust. Machine-learning hazard maps can look convincing and still fail outside the conditions they were trained on. My work benchmarks deep-learning and ensemble models, explains them with SHAP, LIME and Accumulated Local Effects, and measures where their predictions stop being reliable, including an area-of-applicability study during a research placement at Curtin University.

Alongside the modelling I work on the step after prediction: linking hazard surfaces to buildings, land use and population, and using multi-criteria decision analysis for siting and corridor risk. As Principal Investigator on an APNIC Foundation fellowship (Nov 2025 to Apr 2026) I led a multi-country study of AI capability gaps across ASEAN, which connects geospatial analytics with digital infrastructure policy.

On the applied side I hold an IBM Data Analyst certificate, have worked as a data analyst intern (SQL, Python, applied statistics) and a geospatial analyst intern (high-resolution imagery), and I am registered as a Professional Technologist with the Malaysia Board of Technologists.
