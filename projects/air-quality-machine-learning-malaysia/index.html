Air quality modelling in Malaysia with machine learning and GIS

Summary

Air-quality stations are sparse, but public-health decisions need maps. This work, carried out as master’s research at Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS, predicts and maps particulate matter (PM10) and ozone in Malaysia with machine learning, geostatistics and regression, using remote-sensing and weather data. It also measures how temperature, wind and humidity drive pollution through the seasons, so the results can support air-quality management in fast-growing cities.

Key results

  • Random forest mapped PM10 hotspots in Selangor with 98% overall accuracy (K-nearest neighbour 96%, naïve Bayes 91%), with the highest concentrations in urban and industrial areas.
  • PM10 was positively correlated with temperature and wind speed and negatively with humidity, and the highest concentrations coincided with warming during the southwest monsoon.
  • Random forest, decision-tree, linear and support vector regression quantified how climatic variables affect ozone concentration (Chemosphere).
  • PM10 was also predicted across Selangor State with machine learning, and across Malaysian cities with kriging and IDW.
