Published (2 journal articles)

Explainable GeoAI for flood susceptibility and urban exposure

Benchmarks machine-learning and deep-learning flood susceptibility models, explains their behaviour with SHAP, LIME and ALE, and links hazard surfaces to buildings, land use and population.

Problem
Flood susceptibility models are usually judged on accuracy alone. Planners also need to know why a model flags a place and what is actually exposed there.
My role
Lead author and modeller (PhD research)
Key contribution
The first application of Accumulated Local Effects (ALE) to environmental susceptibility modelling, alongside SHAP and LIME, and an explainable framework that non-specialists can read.
Outcome
Two Q1 journal articles. The Sustainable Development paper sits at rank 1 of 65 in the Web of Science Development Studies category.
  • Python
  • scikit-learn
  • TensorFlow and Keras
  • XGBoost
  • SHAP

Manuscripts under review

Reliable GeoAI with the area of applicability

Identifies the feature-space and geographic conditions under which flood susceptibility predictions become unreliable, so maps can show where not to trust them.

Problem
A model trained in one place is often applied in another. Without a check on transferability, predictions in unfamiliar conditions look just as confident as those in familiar ones.
My role
Researcher (visiting research associate, Curtin University)
Key contribution
An area-of-applicability framework for ensemble flood susceptibility models that marks where predictions should not be trusted, in a rapidly urbanising city.
Outcome
Two manuscripts are under review (Geo-spatial Information Science; Applied Water Science). A critical review of validation practice is under review at Environmental Science and Policy. No published results yet.
  • Ensemble and hybrid machine learning
  • Area of applicability (AoA)
  • Uncertainty analysis
  • Python

Published

Two decades of urban flood dynamics from Earth observation

Classifies two decades of land cover in Google Earth Engine and links land-cover change statistically to shifting flood risk with explainable GeoAI.

Problem
Cities change faster than hazard maps are updated. Quantifying how land-cover change has shifted flood risk needs consistent satellite records over long periods.
My role
Lead author
Key contribution
A workflow that goes from two decades of satellite imagery to classified land cover, quantified change and a statistical link between that change and flood risk.
Outcome
Published in Earth Systems and Environment (Q1, 2025). A related earth-observation paper on land use in conflict-affected north-east Nigeria was published in Land Use Policy.
  • Google Earth Engine
  • Multi-temporal satellite imagery
  • Land-cover classification
  • Change detection
  • Explainable machine learning

In progress (review submitted)

Urban heat exposure and vulnerability

A systematic review of urban heat vulnerability assessment and an empirical heat exposure study, both built on remote sensing and machine learning.

Problem
Urban heat maps usually stop at temperature. Understanding who is vulnerable, and what follows from exposure, needs better methods than most current studies use.
My role
Researcher
Key contribution
A review of how heat exposure and social vulnerability have been characterised with remote sensing and machine learning, and where current methods remain weak, paired with an empirical exposure study built entirely in Google Earth Engine.
Outcome
The systematic review has been submitted to Global Challenges. The empirical study is under development. No results are reported here yet.
  • Google Earth Engine
  • Land surface temperature
  • Vegetation indices
  • Built-up surface data
  • Python

Mapping AI capability disparities across ASEAN

A funded, multi-country study of AI capability gaps across ASEAN institutions, leading to evidence-based recommendations for a regional policy roadmap.

Problem
Countries in Southeast Asia are adopting AI at very different speeds. Policy to close the gap needs evidence on which institutional barriers matter most.
My role
Principal Investigator
Key contribution
Led the research, supervised two research assistants, and linked geospatial analytics with AI governance and digital infrastructure policy.
Outcome
USD 10,000 APNIC Foundation research fellowship. A systematic review of disparities, drivers and policy alignment is under review at Humanities and Social Sciences Communications. I also spoke as an expert panellist on responsible AI integration at the International Higher Education Policy Dialogue (March 2026).

Published

Air quality modelling in Malaysia with machine learning and GIS

Predicts and maps PM10 and ozone across Malaysia with machine learning, geostatistics and regression, and analyses their climatic drivers.

Problem
Air-quality monitoring stations are sparse, and pollutant levels depend on weather and season. Decision-makers need spatial predictions and to know what drives them.
My role
Researcher (MSc thesis and graduate research assistant)
Key contribution
Compared machine-learning and geostatistical models for spatial PM10 prediction, and quantified how climatic variables and seasonality influence PM10 and ozone.
Outcome
Four Q1 journal articles published during the assistantship, plus conference papers. Awarded a Graduate Research Scholarship and a full conference travel grant.
  • Python
  • XGBoost
  • Random forest
  • K-nearest neighbours
  • Naïve Bayes

Published

Spatial decision support with multi-criteria analysis

Applies GIS and multi-criteria decision analysis to flood susceptibility, highway landslide risk and landfill siting, turning spatial data into ranked, defensible options.

Problem
Siting and risk decisions involve several competing criteria. Decision-makers need a transparent way to combine spatial layers into a ranking they can defend.
My role
Lead or contributing author
Key contribution
Ensemble fuzzy MCDM for flood susceptibility, a comparative assessment of data mining, MCDM and fuzzy computing, and GIS-based MCDM for highway landslide proneness and landfill suitability.
Outcome
Four Q1 journal articles, from the 2020 Natural Hazards flood susceptibility paper to the 2024 landfill suitability study in Lagos.
  • ArcGIS Pro
  • QGIS
  • Multi-criteria decision making (MCDM)
  • Fuzzy methods
  • Remote sensing
