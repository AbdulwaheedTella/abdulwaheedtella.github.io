What I work on

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Civil Engineering at Monash University Malaysia, working on geospatial artificial intelligence (GeoAI) for flood susceptibility and urban exposure. My first degree is in remote sensing and GIS (FUTA, Nigeria) and my master's is in civil and environmental engineering (Universiti Teknologi PETRONAS), where I modelled air pollution in Malaysia with machine learning.

The problem I keep returning to is trust. Machine-learning hazard maps can look convincing and still fail outside the conditions they were trained on. My work benchmarks deep-learning and ensemble models, explains them with SHAP, LIME and Accumulated Local Effects, and measures where their predictions stop being reliable, including an area-of-applicability study during a research placement at Curtin University.

Research themes

Research overview →
  • Explainable and reliable GeoAI Machine-learning hazard maps are only useful if you can see why they say what they say, and know where they stop being right.
  • Flood and hazard risk modelling Finding which places are most likely to flood or slide, and what sits in harm's way.
  • Large-scale Earth observation Using decades of satellite images to measure how land, heat and air quality change.
  • Urban climate and heat Understanding which people and neighbourhoods are most exposed to urban heat, and how well current methods capture it.
  • Air quality and environmental monitoring Predicting and mapping air pollution from weather, land cover and monitoring-station data.
  • Decision support and AI policy Turning model outputs, and questions about AI readiness, into choices that planners and policymakers can act on.
