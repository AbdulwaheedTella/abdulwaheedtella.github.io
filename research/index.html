Research

Research themes

My work sits around one question, how to make geospatial machine learning reliable enough to inform real decisions. Each theme below links to the papers and projects behind it.

  • Flood and hazard risk modelling
  • Large-scale Earth observation

Explainable and reliable GeoAI

Machine-learning hazard maps are only useful if you can see why they say what they say, and know where they stop being right.

I compare machine-learning and deep-learning models for spatial prediction, explain them with SHAP, LIME and Accumulated Local Effects (including the first application of ALE to environmental susceptibility modelling), and test model transferability using the area of applicability, so that predictions outside the training conditions are flagged instead of trusted.

  • SHAP
  • LIME
  • Accumulated Local Effects
  • Area of applicability
  • Uncertainty quantification

Flood and hazard risk modelling

Finding which places are most likely to flood or slide, and what sits in harm's way.

Flood susceptibility mapping with ensemble, fuzzy and deep-learning models across Nigerian and Malaysian cities, extended from "where is risk high" to "what is exposed" by linking hazard surfaces to buildings, land use and population. Related work covers landslide proneness along a highway corridor and sea-level-rise estimation.

Large-scale Earth observation

Using decades of satellite images to measure how land, heat and air quality change.

Multi-temporal satellite analysis in Google Earth Engine: two decades of land-cover change linked statistically to shifting flood risk, land-use dynamics in conflict-affected north-east Nigeria, and aerosol and land-cover relationships in Lagos.

Urban climate and heat

Understanding which people and neighbourhoods are most exposed to urban heat, and how well current methods capture it.

An empirical urban heat exposure study built entirely in Google Earth Engine (land surface temperature, vegetation indices and built-up surface data for a rapidly urbanising region), plus a systematic review of urban heat vulnerability assessment with remote sensing and machine learning. This is work in progress; the review is submitted and the empirical study is under development.

  • Thermal remote sensing
  • Land surface temperature
  • Vegetation indices
  • Systematic review

Air quality and environmental monitoring

Decision support and AI policy
